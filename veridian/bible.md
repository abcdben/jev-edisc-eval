## The company

**Veridian Orthopedics, Inc.** HQ: 4400 Silver Lake Blvd, Warsaw, Indiana. ~1,800
employees. Founded 1994. Public since 2011 (NYSE: VRDO). Makes joint
reconstruction implants: hips (ApexHip, ClassicHip), knees (KneeFlex, KneeFlex
Revision), shoulders (ShoulderPro). Manufacturing in Warsaw IN and Cork,
Ireland. Regional sales offices in Atlanta, Dallas, Denver, Newark.

Email domain: `@veridianortho.com`. Internal chat: Microsoft Teams. Document
management: "Agile" PLM for design history; "TrackWise" for complaints/CAPA;
"Salesforce" for CRM; "Workday" for HR; "Concur" for expenses. Ticketing: "Jira
Service Desk" for IT.

Internal shorthand people actually use: *Apex* (the product), *the cup*
(acetabular component), *the stem*, *MoM* (metal-on-metal), *Co/Cr* (cobalt /
chromium ions), *ARMD* (adverse reaction to metal debris), *KBT* (Kinetic Bearing

*MDR* (medical device report to FDA), *CAPA*, *PMS* (post-market surveillance),
*NJR* (UK National Joint Registry), *AJRR* (American Joint Replacement
Registry), *KOL* (key opinion leader surgeon), *W&B* (Whitfield & Barr, outside

## The product

**ApexHip** — a metal-on-metal total hip system: a Co/Cr femoral head and stem
plus a Co/Cr acetabular cup with the "KinetiCoat" porous titanium coating for
bone ingrowth (coating acquired with KBT in 2016). Cleared via 510(k) K162447 in
March 2017, predicate ClassicHip. Launched US April 2017, EU (CE) Sept 2017.
Marketed toward "active adults" 45–65: longevity, low wear, large head
diameter for stability. List price ~$9,800 per system. Peak year 2020: ~14,200
systems, ~$96M revenue (about 22% of company revenue).

Sizes: cups 44–62 mm; heads 36–50 mm. Lot numbers: cups `V-2xxx`, stems
`S-7xxx`, heads `H-3xxx`.

**The recall** (narrow issue, RFP 1): cup lots **V-2200 through V-2299**,
manufactured Cork, Nov 2021–Feb 2022, KinetiCoat adhesion failures found in
incoming inspection at two hospitals (coating flaking on unwrapping). Voluntary
Class II recall initiated 14 June 2022; FDA recall number Z-1418-2022; 1,140
units distributed, 412 implanted. Root cause: plasma-spray parameter drift after
a chamber maintenance event. This is a *coating/manufacturing* problem and is
distinct from the *metal-ion/wear* problem that drives the MDL, though
plaintiffs try to blur them and internal emails sometimes do too.

**The MDL issue**: elevated cobalt/chromium ion levels and ARMD (pseudotumors,
tissue necrosis) leading to early revision. Signals: a 2019 internal wear-test
re-analysis (Halvorsen) showing edge-loading wear at high cup inclination
angles; a **March 2021 NJR annual report** flagging ApexHip's 5-year revision
rate at 7.1% vs 3.2% class average; AJRR data in late 2021 pointing the same
way; a **Sept 2021 "Registry Signal Review"** meeting where the company decided
to continue selling with a labeling update rather than withdraw. Sales
continued until a **voluntary market withdrawal announced 2 Feb 2024** after the
MDL was formed (Dec 2023).

**Other products** (distractors; NOT responsive unless ApexHip is also
discussed): KneeFlex (a strong product, launched 2019, its own minor
complaints), ClassicHip (legacy metal-on-polyethylene hip, being phased out),
ShoulderPro.

## Document types and style guide

Distribution across corpus (approximate): email 55%, email thread with quoted
history 15% of those emails, Teams chat 12%, memo/report 8%, meeting minutes/
agenda 5%, spreadsheet summary (described in text, with a small table) 5%,
letter (FDA, hospital, counsel, carrier) 5%, form (complaint form, MDR, hotline
intake, HR form) 5%, calendar invite 3%, text messages 2%.

Rules:
- **Headers**: emails carry `From:`, `To:`, `Cc:` (when any), `Date:` (e.g. `Tue, 14 Sep 2021 08:12`), `Subject:`. Replies keep `RE:`/`FW:` and include quoted history with `-----Original Message-----` or `On ... wrote:`. Teams chats: `[2021-09-14 08:12] Tom Halvorsen: ...` lines. Texts: `Sarah Kim (3:41 PM): ...`.
- **Length**: chats 3–12 lines; emails 40–350 words; memos/reports 300–900 words; minutes 200–500 words; forms 150–400 words.
- **Realism**: typos in chats and phone emails ("Sent from my iPhone"), inconsistent capitalization, people cc'ing the wrong person, "see attached" with the attachment described by filename only (`Apex_wear_55deg_rev2.xlsx`), out-of-office replies, calendar acceptances. People disagree, hedge, and sometimes don't answer the question.
- **Buried content**: in ~20% of threads, the responsive content is in quoted history or the last message of a long thread, not the top.
- **Distractors**: Steinberg's "privileged & confidential" banners appear on both responsive and non-responsive docs; the word "recall" appears in KneeFlex and in "recall the meeting"; "envelope", "cup", "revision" (of documents) and "reserve" (a conference room) appear in innocent contexts.
- **No meta**: never mention RFPs, the benchmark, labels, or that anything is fictional. No placeholders like [NAME].
- **Consistency**: use the cast, dates, lot numbers, and facts above. Invent minor characters freely (nurses, hospital purchasing, agency staff) but keep them consistent within a thread.

## Gray-area guidance for the planner

A document is **gray** when a competent reviewer could reasonably code it either
way. Give every gray doc a one-sentence rationale. Typical gray patterns:
- Mentions Apex and a covered topic in one line inside an otherwise unrelated doc (e.g. a company budget with an Apex reserve line: gray for 13).
- Discusses a covered topic for a *different* product but references Apex by analogy ("same as we did on Apex").
- Scheduling/logistics *about* a responsive meeting with no substance ("moving the Registry Signal Review to 3pm").
- A rep forwarding an NJR news article with "fyi" and no comment (gray for 9).
- HR docs for a person in scope but about an unrelated matter (parking violation).
- A surgeon dinner with no business content (gray for 7 if Feld/Rao present).

Assign the gold label the way a careful, slightly inclusive reviewer would,
flag `gray: true`, and explain.
