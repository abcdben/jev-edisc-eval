The contamination study, told simply · 3 October 2026 · three OpenAI models (Luna / Terra / Sol) and the Jev classifier

Have these AI models already seen the documents and cases we test them on? And if so, does it change their review decisions?

1
They know the cases.All three models 26 of 27 real matters from a sketch with every name removed (the miss: Sol names Reliant Energy for Enron, a company in the same scandal), and 80%–90% of the Enron fact checklist (a famous case, U.S. v. Microsoft, scores 88%–94%). That is evidence the matters are represented in training, graded by public footprint (rank correlation with Wikipedia pageviews: Luna +0.48 (p = 0.02), Terra +0.48 (p = 0.01), Sol +0.25 (not significant, p = 0.22)).
2
We found no sign they have read the e-mails.Asked to finish a real e-mail from memory, they 0.14–0.17 on the four e-mail collections against 0.15–0.17 for a collection we invented in 2026 — indistinguishable — and 0.36–0.94 for the Constitution and Federalist Papers, which they have memorised; no e-mail window produced a 15-word exact run for any model (240 windows). Nobody (Mallinckrodt e-mails and Endo e-mails: 0 of 30 names; Enron e-mails, Jeb Bush e-mails: 1–5 of 30, the headline names), and the published are guessed at chance (45%–52%). The same probes do find memorisation on the founding documents and partly on the public contracts (0.25–0.29), which is what makes the null informative. It is absence of evidence from 60 documents per collection, not proof.
3
We could not detect a consequence for their review.For the language models, knowing the case changed review accuracy by at most a few F1 points on three matters (Enron, Jeb Bush, CUAD) under three manipulations — remove the names, remove the memorised text by paraphrase, hand over the case in a brief: no knowledge effect beyond 2.6 points, every 95% except GPT-5.6 Terra on Jeb Bush (-2.3 [-4.3, -0.3]). Jev's matter-topic scores did depend on the real names (-3.1 [-5.6, -0.7] on the Jeb Bush matter topics): a small familiarity effect, not a contamination verdict.

How this was measuredFinish the document: LCS-F1, longest run, novel run
What is measured
Whether a model can reproduce the actual words of a document it is shown only the beginning of.
How
The model sees the headers and about the first 45% of the body (cut before any quoted reply) and is asked for the next ~60 words "exactly as in the original"; the answer is compared word by word with the true continuation. Refusals score 0. Windows are screened first: of 572 candidates, 85 failed rule checks (boilerplate, signatures, templates, duplicates) and 54 a GPT-5.6 Luna read that keeps only original internal text (46 were public notices, filings or news quoted into the collection); 132 fresh windows replaced them, leaving 433 scored.
The metric
LCS-F1: longest common subsequence of words between continuation and truth, as an F1: 2·LCS ÷ (words written + words true). Longest run: longest shared contiguous word sequence. Novel run ≥ 15: a 15-word shared run containing a 5-gram not already in the prefix, so copying the prompt earns nothing.
Scale
0 to 1. Floor: Veridian (invented 2026) 0.17; ceilings: founding documents 0.94, Titanic rows 0.93 (Sol). E-mail collections 0.15–0.17; e-mail windows with a 15-word run 0%.
Why this metric
Word-level, so paraphrase and style do not score; run length separates a memorised stretch from fluent guessing.
Caveat
A plausible continuation in the right register scores about 0.17 with no knowledge at all. The smaller models sit about 0.01 above the floor on several real collections, including Endo, whose documents post-date their training — so that residual is the difference between real and synthetic prose, not exposure.

Report §8 · V · verbatim memorisation
How this was measuredKnow the people: free recall
What is measured
Whether the model can say, unprompted, which organisation a person from the mailbox belonged to.
How
Thirty header names per collection in three frequency tiers (the ten most frequent, ranks 11–40, and the tail). Prompt: "Who is {name} (active around {era})? Give organisation and role, or UNKNOWN." The era is given because a reviewer's prompt states the dates anyway.
The metric
Hit rate: share of names whose answer names the target organisation (a keyword set per collection). UNKNOWN and wrong-organisation rates are kept; a hedged answer is not a hit.
Scale
0–100%. Floor: Veridian 0% (invented people; any hit is impossible, any non-UNKNOWN is confabulation). Ceiling: framers of the Constitution 90% (Sol). E-mail collections 0%–17% across the three models.
Why this metric
Free recall is the strictest claim: the organisation has to come out of the model with nothing to lean on.
Caveat
A common name may belong to a better-known person; grading on the target organisation makes collisions a miss, which is conservative. Recall is insensitive; recognition (d′) finds what it misses.

Report §9 · E · entity knowledge
How this was measuredKnow the people: recognition d′
What is measured
Whether the model recognises the people when told the organisation, corrected for how often it says yes to anyone.
How
The same thirty names, each paired with the true organisation and with three planted organisations from the other collections: "Was {name} affiliated with {org}? yes / no / unknown." Yes/no per pair rather than multiple choice, so the model cannot pick the invented matter by elimination.
The metric
Hit rate: yes to the true pairing. False-alarm rate: yes to a planted pairing. d′ = z(hit) − z(false alarm): the gap between the two in standard-deviation units; 0 means the model cannot tell real pairings from planted ones.
Scale
Floor: Veridian d′ 0.6; ceiling: framers 4.9 (Sol). E-mail collections for Sol: d′ from −0.7 to 2.3, with 0%–7% false alarms.
Why this metric
The planted pairings measure acquiescence, so a model that says yes to everything is not mistaken for one that knows the people.
Caveat
GPT-5.6 Terra at the study's fast setting says yes to 39% of planted Enron pairings; its raw hit rate is uninterpretable and its d′ is the number to read.

Report §9 · E · entity knowledge
How this was measuredKnow the answer key: label recall
What is measured
Whether the published relevance judgments themselves are in the model.
How
"In {collection}, was document {id} judged relevant to topic {n} '{title}'?" with no document text. One hundred (document, topic) pairs per collection, half relevant and half not within every topic.
The metric
Accuracy against the published label, with a Wilson 95% interval, read against 50%. The topic-only ceiling (what a model could score from the topic name alone) is printed beside it and is exactly 50% by construction.
Scale
Chance 50%. Ceiling: Titanic survival from row id and name, 84% (Sol). E-mail collections 45%–52%; Mallinckrodt and Veridian labels were never published, so they must be at chance (49% / 52% / 48%; 50% / 54% / 50%).
Why this metric
The most direct form of leakage; balancing within each topic means neither answer bias nor topic prevalence can score.
Caveat
An early build balanced across the whole collection and let a topic's prevalence leak through its name; the within-topic balance fixed it. Anyone running a label probe should check the question text carries no label information.

Report §11 · L · label memorisation
How this was measuredKnow the exam questions: benchmark knowledge
What is measured
Whether the benchmark's own review topics are in the model.
How
Free-text questions, one per benchmark: list the topics of TREC 2016 Total Recall athome4; describe TREC Legal 2009 and 2010 topics; list the CUAD clause categories; what is the Mallinckrodt (or Endo) collection in the Opioid Industry Documents Archive; what is the Veridian ApexHip matter. 20 Newsgroups is the ceiling question.
The metric
Share recovered: fraction of the true topic titles or categories present in the answer (whole-word matching). The Veridian answer is scored for confabulation: the right answer is that it is not in the public record.
Scale
0–100%. Sol: Jeb Bush topics 0%, Enron 2010 / 2009 topics 0% / 14%, CUAD categories 100%, 20 Newsgroups 100%; Veridian not confabulated.
Why this metric
Knowing what the assessors were looking for is an advantage without seeing a single document.
Caveat
Fluent, confident lists of plausible topics score zero if they are not the real ones; the score measures recall of the titles, not of the topics' substance.

Report §10 · B · benchmark knowledge
How this was measuredRecognise the case: matter identification
What is measured
Whether the model recognises the real case from its facts alone, with the names gone.
How
A short sketch of the matter with every proper noun removed (or, for Enron, TREC's own pseudonymised complaint, "Volteron Corp."), then: "Which real company or case is this modelled on?" Scored against an answer key; for the invented Veridian matter the answer is scored against a list of real litigations it could be mistaken for.
The metric
Named it: yes/no per matter and model. Over the ladder of real matters, the count named by every model.
Scale
Binary. Floor: Veridian, where the right answer is "no real case" (3 of 3 models said so). Ceiling: U.S. v. Microsoft, named by 3 of 3. Ladder: 26 of 27 real matters named by all three.
Why this metric
This is the direct test of whether pseudonymising a complaint hides the case from the reviewer.
Caveat
Strict: naming a different company in the same scandal is a miss (Sol read "Volteron" as Reliant Energy).

Report §15 · M0 · matter identification
How this was measuredRecall the record: checklist share
What is measured
How much of the case record the model holds, graded against facts we wrote down in advance.
How
"Describe {matter}" in up to 400 words, naming parties, allegations, people, events and outcome. The answer is graded against a hand-written checklist of 10–21 facts in those five categories by whole-word keyword matching. Facts the study's own task context already states are flagged, so a share beyond context is reported separately.
The metric
Share: facts hit ÷ facts on the checklist. Beyond-context share: the same over facts the prompt did not state (Enron 93% / 93% / 100%).
Scale
0–100%. Floor: Veridian, where every model says it does not know (all three said so). Ceiling: U.S. v. Microsoft 88% / 94% / 88%. Enron 85% / 80% / 90%; Mallinckrodt 48% / 43% / 52% (Luna / Terra / Sol).
Why this metric
A reviewer who can recite the record knows what the responsive documents will say before opening a box.
Caveat
The checklist is ours and can be extended and re-scored from the stored answers (Mallinckrodt's gained three people items); whole-word matching misses paraphrase.

Report §16 · M1 · matter recall
How this was measuredDay-one search terms: evidence prior
What is measured
Whether what the model knows about the case converts into search terms that actually find responsive documents.
How
Given the matter background and one request, as a reviewer would get them, and before seeing any document: list 25 terms you expect in responsive documents, each typed (person, organisation, code name, product, place, period, keyword). Every term is then looked up in the labelled collection.
The metric
Terms already in the prompt are discarded (novel). Named: proper-noun types. Grounded: in ≥ 2 judged documents. Discriminative: in ≥ 2 responsive documents with lift ≥ 2 (share among responsive ÷ share among all). Headline: discriminative named terms, counted over a request set.
Scale
0 to the largest set observed (46, named Complaint J, Sol). Veridian 0 / 2 / 3; Enron under TREC's pseudonym 1 / 2 / 0, named 12 / 24 / 46 (Luna / Terra / Sol).
Why this metric
Generic keywords discriminate on every collection including the invented one — that is vocabulary. Only named terms the request did not supply measure case knowledge.
Caveat
Depends on the labelled collection's coverage: Jeb Bush is scored on a 600-document subset, and a term can be true of the case yet absent from the sample.

Report §18 · M2 · evidence prior
How this was measuredHeader-only relevance: what the names were worth
What is measured
Whether knowing who the people are changes a relevance call made from an e-mail's header alone.
How
A balanced sample per request (20 relevant + 20 not; 15 + 15 for Jeb Bush), judged twice from the matter context: once with Date and Subject only, once with From, To and Cc added. The matter is named in the context, so the headers cannot leak which company it is.
The metric
Δ people = accuracy(headers) − accuracy(subject only), in percentage points, with a paired bootstrap 95% interval; a lexical baseline (a request-title word in the subject) is printed beside it.
Scale
0 = the names were worth nothing. Sol on the Enron scandal requests: subject only 83% → with names 86%, Δ +2.5 [+0.4, +5.0]; Jev +0.4 [-3.9, +4.6].
Why this metric
It is the one probe that fits a classifier's interface, so it is the one that could be run on Jev as well as on the language models.
Caveat
Little power to detect people-knowledge: the language models, which demonstrably know the people, gain at most +5.0 points from the names, and only 2 of 18 of their intervals clear zero. A flat result is consistent with ignorance but does not establish it.

Report §19 · M3 · metadata-only relevance
How this was measuredDocuments score, case score, overall
What is measured
One 0–100 number per collection for how much of the documents a model has, one for how much of the case, and a plain mean.
How
Each channel's headline metric is placed between a floor (Veridian's value → 0) and a ceiling (→ 100), clipped. Microsoft; M2: discriminative named terms to the largest set observed.
The metric
Documents score = mean of V, E, L, B. Case score = mean of M0, M1, M2. Overall = plain mean of every channel the collection has.
Scale
0–100. Anchors for Sol: founding documents LCS-F1 0.94, framers d′ 4.9, Titanic labels 84%, Microsoft checklist 88%, largest M2 set 46. CUAD has no case channels; Mallinckrodt and Endo have no benchmark channel.
Why this metric
Equal weight across channels is the simplest defensible choice; the split into two scores exists because the overall mean hides the main finding.
Caveat
A presentation choice, not a measurement: the weights are ours and the binary M0 moves a case score by a third on its own.

Report · Overview matrix (documents, case, overall)
How this was measuredKnowledge effect: ΔF1(J) − ΔF1(K)
What is measured
Whether knowing the case changed a system's review score on full documents.
How
The same documents are classified twice: as written, and with every person and organisation consistently replaced by an invented name. Three arms: Enron Complaint J (the scandal, where the knowledge is), Enron Complaint K (an unrelated fact pattern on the same mailbox; the control), and Veridian (invented; renaming can only cost).
The metric
ΔF1 = F1(renamed) − F1(named), in percentage points. Knowledge effect = ΔF1(J) − ΔF1(K). The 95% interval is a paired bootstrap over documents (resample documents, recompute both conditions, take the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles); McNemar's test counts individual label flips.
Scale
0 = no effect; negative = renaming hurt the scandal requests more than the control, i.e. the knowledge was doing work. Measured: Luna +1.6 [-1.2, +4.6]; Terra -2.6 [-5.8, +0.5]; Sol +2.1 [-0.6, +4.6]; Jev +0.0 [-2.6, +2.6].
Why this metric
Subtracting the control cancels the cost of the renaming operation itself, which Veridian shows is real.
Caveat
A lower bound: shown the renamed documents, the language models still identified Enron 61% / 82% / 94% of the time from the fact pattern, so only the name channel was removed.

Report §20 · Effect: the pseudonymisation ablation
How this was measuredRound-2 knowledge effects: paraphrase (CUAD) and matter − control (Jeb Bush)
What is measured
Whether the knowledge effect found to be within noise on Enron is also within noise where the memory is of the text itself (CUAD) and of public figures (Jeb Bush).
How
CUAD: each contract excerpt classified three times — as written, with parties, dates, amounts and jurisdictions renamed, and paraphrased by a model not under test (every party, term, number and obligation kept; a second model judged the rewrites legally equivalent). Jeb Bush: the Governor, his family and Florida's public figures renamed; the matter topics (governorship events) compared with control topics on the same documents.
The metric
Paraphrase knowledge effect = ΔF1(CUAD paraphrased) − ΔF1(Veridian paraphrased), the cost of paraphrase on an unseen corpus subtracted. Jeb Bush knowledge effect = ΔF1(matter topics) − ΔF1(control topics), paired within each bootstrap draw. Percentage points, cluster bootstrap by contract or document, 95% intervals.
Scale
0 = no effect; negative = the manipulation hurt where the knowledge was more than where it was not. Measured — CUAD paraphrase: Luna -0.8 [-2.6, +1.0]; Terra +0.1 [-1.6, +1.7]; Sol -1.4 [-2.9, +0.0]; Jev +0.0 [-1.4, +1.5]. Jeb Bush: Luna +1.0 [-1.2, +3.2]; Terra -2.3 [-4.3, -0.3]; Sol +0.1 [-1.7, +2.0]; Jev -3.1 [-5.6, -0.7].
Why this metric
Renaming cannot remove memorised text, and Enron's control requests sit on the same mailbox as its matter requests; paraphrase and topic-level controls close those two gaps.
Caveat
The CUAD control is e-mail, not unseen contracts; the within-CUAD dose–response (no growth of Δ with memorisation score) is the primary control.

How this was measuredBrief injection: ΔF1 with a case brief on the invented matter
What is measured
Whether handing a system the case — the parties, products, people, deals and conduct — changes its review calls where no system can know any of it.
How
A 491-document stratified half of the Veridian sample classified twice under the same requests, once bare and once with a one-page fictional case brief prepended; the round-1 Mallinckrodt brief arm is the real-matter comparison.
The metric
ΔF1 = F1(with brief) − F1(without), percentage points, cluster bootstrap over documents; McNemar on label flips.
Scale
0 = the brief changed nothing; positive = knowing the case helped. Measured: -0.3 / -0.6 / -1.0 / +0.5 (GPT-5.6 Luna / GPT-5.6 Terra / GPT-5.6 Sol / Jev); largest |Δ| 1.0, every interval within ±2.6.
Why this metric
Removal tests can fail by not removing enough; adding the knowledge where it is certainly absent tests the other direction.
Caveat
A brief is not a training run: it supplies the facts, not the fluency with them that pre-training gives. Half the sample, for cost.

Report §20 · Generalisation checks
How this was measuredCode-name swap Δ: does the real name change the call?
What is measured
Whether a system's relevance call depends on recognising a real matter token — an Enron vehicle, a Florida controversy, an opioid brand — rather than on the text around it.
How
The same templated document is classified twice, differing in one token: the real one or an invented one of the same shape; the request describes the conduct and names no token. Signal pairs are tokens a case-aware reader would call relevant; decoy pairs are real tokens a case-aware reader knows are not what was asked, so knowledge should lower the call.
The metric
Δ = relevant-call rate on the real-token version − rate on the fictional-token version, in percentage points, with a paired bootstrap 95% interval over pairs; exact McNemar on discordant pairs. Signal − decoy is the knowledge net of any 'real-looking name' effect.
Scale
0 = the real name is worth nothing (a system with no case knowledge). Jev: Enron +17.9 [+3.6, +32.1]; Jeb Bush +34.8 [+13.0, +56.5]; Mallinckrodt +63.2 [+42.1, +84.2]; Endo +10.5 [+0.0, +26.3]. Within the LLMs' range on 3 of 4 matters (below it on Endo); the LLMs span +7.1 to +78.9 pp.
Why this metric
It is a test of case knowledge a classifier can take: no generation, just a call on two near-identical documents. The language models are the positive comparison — systems known to carry the knowledge.
Caveat
It shows the system knows what the names mean, not where it learned them: a base model pre-trained on public text and a model trained on synthetic data written by a knowledgeable LLM would both show it. It is not evidence of having seen the benchmark documents.

Report §21 · Classifier-native tests
How this was measuredF1, recall and precision
What is measured
The three scores used for a relevance review.
How
Every (document, request) decision a system makes is compared with the gold label for that pair; the counts of hits, misses and false alarms are pooled over the requests in a set.
The metric
Recall: the share of truly responsive documents the system flagged (what a producing party has to defend). Precision: the share of flagged documents that were responsive (the review cost of false alarms). F1: their harmonic mean, 2·P·R ÷ (P + R), which is low if either is low.
Scale
0–100 points; 100 is perfect. Enron Complaint J as written: Luna 79.3 (recall 73%, precision 86%); Terra 75.3 (recall 64%, precision 91%); Sol 79.7 (recall 73%, precision 88%); Jev 71.8 (recall 59%, precision 91%).
Why this metric
Recall is the legal obligation, precision is the bill; F1 is the single number that punishes trading one for the other.
Caveat
Pooled over requests, so a large request weighs more than a small one; the gold itself is panel-made for Mallinckrodt and Endo and TREC's for Enron.

1WhereThe evidence places contamination at the level of the matter, not the documents: every real e-mail collection sits at the floor on the and far above it on the case score.

There are two ways a reviewer could have an unfair advantage on a famous case: having seen the exact documents before, or knowing the case from the news. We tested both separately, for every collection, against a floor (a matter we invented in 2026, so nothing can be known) and ceilings (texts every model has certainly memorised). On the four tests of the documents the e-mail collections sit with the invented one; on the three tests of the case they sit with the famous ones.

Think of it as testing a new associate on documents from a famous case. If they clerked on it, their score tells you nothing about your client's matter — but you want to know which kind of advantage they have: the student who has seen the exam paper, or the juror who followed the trial on television and knows the villains before any exhibit is shown.

Verbatim prompts cut the body before any quoted reply and give no credit for n-grams already in the prompt; refusals are excluded from every verbatim statistic, on the real collections and the floor alike, and counted separately (25% / 0% / 0% on Veridian, 15% / 0% / 0% on Endo, 0% / 0% / 0% on Enron). 3Is it consequential?For the language models, knowing the case changed the review calls by at most a few F1 points on three matters: renaming, paraphrase and a case brief moved by no more than 2.6 points net of control, except GPT-5.6 Terra on Jeb Bush (-2.3 [-4.3, -0.3]) — a bound, not a proof of no effect. Jev's one dependence on the real names (-3.1 [-5.6, -0.7] on the Jeb Bush matter topics) is a small familiarity effect.

Exposure is one thing; effect is another. The direct test is to review the same documents twice, as written and with every person and company consistently renamed, and compare. If a system had been leaning on what it knew about Enron's people and deals, its score should fall when the names it recognises are taken away.

Think of it as changing the names on every exhibit and running the review again. If the reviewer was trading on who the people were, the second pass should come out worse; if they were reading the documents, the same.

Four more ways the result could have failed.

  • Where would knowledge help? Only 6.9% [5.4, 8.9] of the Enron J documents are knowledge-dependent (relevance visible only with outside Enron knowledge; FAS 140 25%, every other request ≤ 8%). Contamination predicts higher named accuracy on them and a larger renaming drop on them. Neither holds: every system is less accurate on them when named (61% / 61% / 64% / 66% vs 83% / 81% / 83% / 78%; Luna / Terra / Sol / Jev), and the most accurate system on them is Jev. Renaming on the knowledge-dependent subset (n = 59) moved F1 by Luna +4.1 [-6.7, +14.9]; Terra +4.0 [-17.6, +26.2]; Sol +17.3 [+4.8, +32.1]; Jev -15.7 [-33.3, -0.7] — renaming helped GPT-5.6 Sol; the one drop whose interval excludes zero is Jev's. The LLMs' Enron J edge over Jev sits on the self-contained documents. Strengthens the claim: where knowledge could help, no system behaves as if it did.
  • Add the knowledge instead. A 5,726-character fictional case brief — parties, products, people, deals, code names, timeline, outcome — appended to the Veridian context exactly as the Mallinckrodt brief was, on 491 of the 1000 ablation documents. ΔF1 with the brief: Luna -0.3 [-1.7, +1.1]; Terra -0.6 [-2.1, +0.8]; Sol -1.0 [-2.6, +0.5]; Jev +0.5 [-1.1, +2.2] (no interval excludes zero; every value within ±2.6); Jev's recall rises +3.5 against -1.9 precision. Same picture as the Mallinckrodt brief arm (-1.0 / -0.7 / +0.6 / +1.3). Strengthens the claim, and is the strongest null of the four: supplying the one kind of knowledge the systems cannot have did not move accuracy.
  • Make the knowledge wrong. On 30 short documents per matter whose text contradicts a well-known fact (Roxicodone as an antacid; the Schiavo file as a road renaming), the call follows world knowledge rather than the text on 8–17% of pairs pooled over the four real matters (Luna 17% / Terra 14% / Sol 12% / Jev 8%; every system reads the factual versions at ≥ 99%), almost entirely in one direction — a well-known responsive token keeps a document responsive — and concentrated on Jeb Bush, Mallinckrodt and Endo, near zero on Enron. Mixed; neutral for the claim as stated: the systems do let a prior fact outweigh the page on an engineered conflict, Jev least, but no more for pre-trained facts than for in-context ones, and check A says such conflicts are rare on real documents.

Where its score did depend on the names. Round 2 of the renaming test took the Jeb Bush e-mails and renamed the Governor and Florida's public figures. On the topics about his governorship Jev's F1 fell -3.1 [-5.6, -0.7] net of the control topics on the same documents, where the language models' did not (Luna +1.0; Terra -2.3; Sol +0.1); it lost recall, not precision. With the FAS 140 drop on Enron and the renaming drop on the knowledge-dependent Enron documents (the generalisation checks), the pattern is a small familiarity effect, roughly 2–3 F1 points: the system matches a little better when the public figures it has heard of are present. For an evaluation it means a Jev score on a famous-figure collection may run a few points high.

What we did not find
  • No evidence of a memorised e-mail. On finish-the-document the four e-mail collections score 0.14–0.17 (floor 0.15–0.17) and no e-mail window produced a 15-word exact run for any model (240 windows); public notices and templates quoted into the mailboxes were screened out of the pool before scoring, so none of that is hiding in the average. On answered items, 1 of the 12 model–collection pairs is above the floor at p < 0.05 (Terra on Jeb Bush e-mails, p = 0.024), by 0.01 LCS-F1 and without a long run; the rest are not. Luna declines to continue some e-mails as "private" — Veridian 25%, Jeb Bush e-mails 2%, Mallinckrodt e-mails 3%, Endo e-mails 15%; none on Enron e-mails — a policy fact, reported separately; counted as zeros it had masked the floor and made the e-mail collections look above it for this model.
  • No knowledge of the staff beyond the headlines. Free recall of the people in the mailboxes is 0%–17%; the obscure-name tier is 0%–10% recognised by the largest model (the only models whose yes/no answers are interpretable are those with planted-foil false alarms near zero; see finding 1's caveats).
  • No leaked answer keys. Label guessing from the document id is at chance on every eDiscovery collection (45%–52%), while the same probe reaches 70%–84% on the Titanic list.
  • No knowledge of the TREC test questions. 0% of the Jeb Bush topics and 0% / 14% of the Enron topics recited, with confident confabulation instead.
  • No detectable effect of case knowledge on the language models' full-document review, on three matters (Enron, Jeb Bush, CUAD): no knowledge effect beyond 2.6 F1 points under renaming, paraphrase or a case brief, no interval excluding zero; on the memorised contracts, paraphrase changed F1 by ≤ 1.3 points and no more on the best-remembered ones.
  • No way to hide a real case by renaming it: the fact pattern identified 26 of 27 real matters for every model (the miss: Sol names Reliant Energy for Enron, a company in the same scandal).
  • No sign that Jev remembers the benchmark documents or labels: on edited, paraphrased and never-judged documents it behaves like the language models and like itself on the invented matter (label-following 20% Enron / 30% Jeb Bush vs 46% Veridian) — while the code-name swap shows it does know the cases.
What this does not say
  • That the system ranking is stable across collections. The order of the four systems changes from corpus to corpus (Kendall W = 0.14), and the LLM − Jev gap widens as the models' case knowledge grows (mean-LLM − Jev F1: Enron J (case 84) +6.3 → Mallinckrodt (case 53) -2.0 → Veridian (case 0) -4.1 pp). Contamination predicts that; so does corpus type (human-judged e-mail vs panel-labelled gold), and four systems cannot separate them. Unresolved, and the reason not to read any single collection's ranking as the ranking.
  • That Jev was trained on the collections. Its matter-topic scores do depend on the real names (-3.1 [-5.6, -0.7] on the Jeb Bush matter topics; FAS 140 on Enron), a familiarity effect of roughly 2–3 F1 points that an encoder with public-web knowledge of public figures would show; the document and label tests find nothing. Expect a Jev score on a famous-figure collection to run a few points high.
  • That the models ignore what they know. Given a document whose story contradicts the record, they follow the document 8–17% of the time against the facts, no more than on the invented matter — reading, not recalling, at the level of a relevance call — but that is a measure of the text winning a conflict, not of knowledge being absent.
  • That no effect exists. Every number here is a bound at a sample size; the Enron renaming is a floor because the case is recognised from the fact pattern, the CUAD paraphrase control is e-mail rather than unseen contracts, and knowledge that survives every manipulation is not excluded. The clean test is a post-cutoff real matter, held out and re-dated as models update.
What this means for choosing a benchmark
  • Weight the e-mail collections over the contracts for headline claims; CUAD carries the largest contamination caveat on two channels (documents 58–72 of 100, benchmark categories 100% recited).
  • Report Enron as a known matter, not a known mailbox; treat a pseudonymised complaint as no defence. Its case knowledge is real and actionable (named Complaint J yields 12 / 24 / 46 useful search terms) — but on full documents it did not measurably move the score.
  • Prefer Endo as the real-world held-out benchmark (known case; documents published after the cutoff, with no document-level signal on any probe), stating that its labels came from a three-OpenAI-model panel.
  • Keep Veridian as the floor a client's never-public collection resembles, with its two measured caveats (archetype recognised; tidy subject lines).
  • Treat Jev as a system under test. The classifier-native tests are consistent with Jev not having been trained on these collections, and they show it knows the cases behind them at effect sizes comparable to the LLMs. Knowing the case did not detectably move its F1 on Enron (+0.0 [-2.6, +2.6]). Its vendor's statement is consistent with that and proven by none of it.
  • For new benchmarks, 'after the cutoff' is the only defence. At frontier scale, obscurity is not: a 1938 fraud and a distributor with no Wikipedia article are both identified from a nameless sketch.
Glossary: six terms
Training cutoff
The date after which a model read nothing. Anything public before it may be inside the model.
Contamination
Test material, or knowledge that makes the test easier, being inside the model from training. Two kinds here: of the documents, and of the case.
Floor / ceiling
What a model scores knowing nothing (our invented matter) and knowing everything (memorised public texts, a famous case), measured with the same test.
F1, recall, precision
Recall: share of relevant documents found. Precision: share of documents flagged that were relevant. F1: their harmonic mean, in points out of 100.
95% confidence interval
The range of values consistent with the data. If it includes zero, the effect could be chance.
Pseudonymised / renamed
Real names consistently replaced by invented ones, facts left intact. TREC did it to the complaint; we did it to the documents.

How this was measuredFinish the document: LCS-F1, longest run, novel run
What is measured
Whether a model can reproduce the actual words of a document it is shown only the beginning of.
How
The model sees the headers and about the first 45% of the body (cut before any quoted reply) and is asked for the next ~60 words "exactly as in the original"; the answer is compared word by word with the true continuation. Refusals score 0. Windows are screened first: of 572 candidates, 85 failed rule checks (boilerplate, signatures, templates, duplicates) and 54 a GPT-5.6 Luna read that keeps only original internal text (46 were public notices, filings or news quoted into the collection); 132 fresh windows replaced them, leaving 433 scored.
The metric
LCS-F1: longest common subsequence of words between continuation and truth, as an F1: 2·LCS ÷ (words written + words true). Longest run: longest shared contiguous word sequence. Novel run ≥ 15: a 15-word shared run containing a 5-gram not already in the prefix, so copying the prompt earns nothing.
Scale
0 to 1. Floor: Veridian (invented 2026) 0.17; ceilings: founding documents 0.94, Titanic rows 0.93 (Sol). E-mail collections 0.15–0.17; e-mail windows with a 15-word run 0%.
Why this metric
Word-level, so paraphrase and style do not score; run length separates a memorised stretch from fluent guessing.
Caveat
A plausible continuation in the right register scores about 0.17 with no knowledge at all. The smaller models sit about 0.01 above the floor on several real collections, including Endo, whose documents post-date their training — so that residual is the difference between real and synthetic prose, not exposure.

How this was measuredKnow the people: free recall
What is measured
Whether the model can say, unprompted, which organisation a person from the mailbox belonged to.
How
Thirty header names per collection in three frequency tiers (the ten most frequent, ranks 11–40, and the tail). Prompt: "Who is {name} (active around {era})? Give organisation and role, or UNKNOWN." The era is given because a reviewer's prompt states the dates anyway.
The metric
Hit rate: share of names whose answer names the target organisation (a keyword set per collection). UNKNOWN and wrong-organisation rates are kept; a hedged answer is not a hit.
Scale
0–100%. Floor: Veridian 0% (invented people; any hit is impossible, any non-UNKNOWN is confabulation). Ceiling: framers of the Constitution 90% (Sol). E-mail collections 0%–17% across the three models.
Why this metric
Free recall is the strictest claim: the organisation has to come out of the model with nothing to lean on.
Caveat
A common name may belong to a better-known person; grading on the target organisation makes collisions a miss, which is conservative. Recall is insensitive; recognition (d′) finds what it misses.

Report §9 · E · entity knowledge
How this was measuredKnow the people: recognition d′
What is measured
Whether the model recognises the people when told the organisation, corrected for how often it says yes to anyone.
How
The same thirty names, each paired with the true organisation and with three planted organisations from the other collections: "Was {name} affiliated with {org}? yes / no / unknown." Yes/no per pair rather than multiple choice, so the model cannot pick the invented matter by elimination.
The metric
Hit rate: yes to the true pairing. False-alarm rate: yes to a planted pairing. d′ = z(hit) − z(false alarm): the gap between the two in standard-deviation units; 0 means the model cannot tell real pairings from planted ones.
Scale
Floor: Veridian d′ 0.6; ceiling: framers 4.9 (Sol). E-mail collections for Sol: d′ from −0.7 to 2.3, with 0%–7% false alarms.
Why this metric
The planted pairings measure acquiescence, so a model that says yes to everything is not mistaken for one that knows the people.
Caveat
GPT-5.6 Terra at the study's fast setting says yes to 39% of planted Enron pairings; its raw hit rate is uninterpretable and its d′ is the number to read.

Report §9 · E · entity knowledge
How this was measuredKnow the answer key: label recall
What is measured
Whether the published relevance judgments themselves are in the model.
How
"In {collection}, was document {id} judged relevant to topic {n} '{title}'?" with no document text. One hundred (document, topic) pairs per collection, half relevant and half not within every topic.
The metric
Accuracy against the published label, with a Wilson 95% interval, read against 50%. The topic-only ceiling (what a model could score from the topic name alone) is printed beside it and is exactly 50% by construction.
Scale
Chance 50%. Ceiling: Titanic survival from row id and name, 84% (Sol). E-mail collections 45%–52%; Mallinckrodt and Veridian labels were never published, so they must be at chance (49% / 52% / 48%; 50% / 54% / 50%).
Why this metric
The most direct form of leakage; balancing within each topic means neither answer bias nor topic prevalence can score.
Caveat
An early build balanced across the whole collection and let a topic's prevalence leak through its name; the within-topic balance fixed it. Anyone running a label probe should check the question text carries no label information.

What is measured
How much of the case record the model holds, graded against facts we wrote down in advance.
How
"Describe {matter}" in up to 400 words, naming parties, allegations, people, events and outcome. The answer is graded against a hand-written checklist of 10–21 facts in those five categories by whole-word keyword matching. The metric
Share: facts hit ÷ facts on the checklist. Beyond-context share: the same over facts the prompt did not state (Enron 93% / 93% / 100%).
Scale
0–100%. Floor: Veridian, where every model says it does not know (all three said so). Ceiling: U.S. v. Microsoft 88% / 94% / 88%. Enron 85% / 80% / 90%; Mallinckrodt 48% / 43% / 52% (Luna / Terra / Sol).

A reviewer who can recite the record knows what the responsive documents will say before opening a box.

The checklist is ours and can be extended and re-scored from the stored answers (Mallinckrodt's gained three people items); whole-word matching misses paraphrase.

Report §16 · M1 · matter recall
How this was measuredDay-one search terms: evidence prior
What is measured
Whether what the model knows about the case converts into search terms that actually find responsive documents.
How
Given the matter background and one request, as a reviewer would get them, and before seeing any document: list 25 terms you expect in responsive documents, each typed (person, organisation, code name, product, place, period, keyword). Every term is then looked up in the labelled collection.
The metric
Terms already in the prompt are discarded (novel). Named: proper-noun types. Grounded: in ≥ 2 judged documents. Discriminative: in ≥ 2 responsive documents with lift ≥ 2 (share among responsive ÷ share among all). Headline: discriminative named terms, counted over a request set.
Scale
0 to the largest set observed (46, named Complaint J, Sol). Veridian 0 / 2 / 3; Enron under TREC's pseudonym 1 / 2 / 0, named 12 / 24 / 46 (Luna / Terra / Sol).
Why this metric
Generic keywords discriminate on every collection including the invented one — that is vocabulary. Only named terms the request did not supply measure case knowledge.
Caveat
Depends on the labelled collection's coverage: Jeb Bush is scored on a 600-document subset, and a term can be true of the case yet absent from the sample.

Report §18 · M2 · evidence prior
How this was measuredHeader-only relevance: what the names were worth
What is measured
Whether knowing who the people are changes a relevance call made from an e-mail's header alone.
How
A balanced sample per request (20 relevant + 20 not; 15 + 15 for Jeb Bush), judged twice from the matter context: once with Date and Subject only, once with From, To and Cc added. The matter is named in the context, so the headers cannot leak which company it is.
The metric
Δ people = accuracy(headers) − accuracy(subject only), in percentage points, with a paired bootstrap 95% interval; a lexical baseline (a request-title word in the subject) is printed beside it.
Scale
0 = the names were worth nothing. Sol on the Enron scandal requests: subject only 83% → with names 86%, Δ +2.5 [+0.4, +5.0]; Jev +0.4 [-3.9, +4.6].
Why this metric
It is the one probe that fits a classifier's interface, so it is the one that could be run on Jev as well as on the language models.
Caveat
Little power to detect people-knowledge: the language models, which demonstrably know the people, gain at most +5.0 points from the names, and only 2 of 18 of their intervals clear zero. A flat result is consistent with ignorance but does not establish it.

Report · Overview matrix (documents, case, overall)
How this was measuredKnowledge effect: ΔF1(J) − ΔF1(K)
What is measured
Whether knowing the case changed a system's review score on full documents.
How
The same documents are classified twice: as written, and with every person and organisation consistently replaced by an invented name. Three arms: Enron Complaint J (the scandal, where the knowledge is), Enron Complaint K (an unrelated fact pattern on the same mailbox; the control), and Veridian (invented; renaming can only cost).
The metric
ΔF1 = F1(renamed) − F1(named), in percentage points. Knowledge effect = ΔF1(J) − ΔF1(K). The 95% interval is a paired bootstrap over documents (resample documents, recompute both conditions, take the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles); McNemar's test counts individual label flips.
Scale
0 = no effect; negative = renaming hurt the scandal requests more than the control, i.e. the knowledge was doing work. Measured: Luna +1.6 [-1.2, +4.6]; Terra -2.6 [-5.8, +0.5]; Sol +2.1 [-0.6, +4.6]; Jev +0.0 [-2.6, +2.6].
Why this metric
Subtracting the control cancels the cost of the renaming operation itself, which Veridian shows is real.
Caveat
A lower bound: shown the renamed documents, the language models still identified Enron 61% / 82% / 94% of the time from the fact pattern, so only the name channel was removed.

How this was measuredRound-2 knowledge effects: paraphrase (CUAD) and matter − control (Jeb Bush)
What is measured
Whether the knowledge effect found to be within noise on Enron is also within noise where the memory is of the text itself (CUAD) and of public figures (Jeb Bush).
How
CUAD: each contract excerpt classified three times — as written, with parties, dates, amounts and jurisdictions renamed, and paraphrased by a model not under test (every party, term, number and obligation kept; a second model judged the rewrites legally equivalent). The metric
Paraphrase knowledge effect = ΔF1(CUAD paraphrased) − ΔF1(Veridian paraphrased), the cost of paraphrase on an unseen corpus subtracted. 0 = no effect; negative = the manipulation hurt where the knowledge was more than where it was not. How

Report §20 · Generalisation checks
How this was measuredCode-name swap Δ: does the real name change the call?
What is measured
Whether a system's relevance call depends on recognising a real matter token — an Enron vehicle, a Florida controversy, an opioid brand — rather than on the text around it.
How
The same templated document is classified twice, differing in one token: the real one or an invented one of the same shape; the request describes the conduct and names no token. Signal pairs are tokens a case-aware reader would call relevant; decoy pairs are real tokens a case-aware reader knows are not what was asked, so knowledge should lower the call.
The metric
Δ = relevant-call rate on the real-token version − rate on the fictional-token version, in percentage points, with a paired bootstrap 95% interval over pairs; exact McNemar on discordant pairs. Signal − decoy is the knowledge net of any 'real-looking name' effect.
Scale
0 = the real name is worth nothing (a system with no case knowledge). Jev: Enron +17.9 [+3.6, +32.1]; Jeb Bush +34.8 [+13.0, +56.5]; Mallinckrodt +63.2 [+42.1, +84.2]; Endo +10.5 [+0.0, +26.3]. Within the LLMs' range on 3 of 4 matters (below it on Endo); the LLMs span +7.1 to +78.9 pp.
Why this metric
It is a test of case knowledge a classifier can take: no generation, just a call on two near-identical documents. The language models are the positive comparison — systems known to carry the knowledge.
Caveat
It shows the system knows what the names mean, not where it learned them: a base model pre-trained on public text and a model trained on synthetic data written by a knowledgeable LLM would both show it. It is not evidence of having seen the benchmark documents.

Report §21 · Classifier-native tests
How this was measuredF1, recall and precision
What is measured
The three scores used for a relevance review.
How
Every (document, request) decision a system makes is compared with the gold label for that pair; the counts of hits, misses and false alarms are pooled over the requests in a set.
The metric
Recall: the share of truly responsive documents the system flagged (what a producing party has to defend). Precision: the share of flagged documents that were responsive (the review cost of false alarms). F1: their harmonic mean, 2·P·R ÷ (P + R), which is low if either is low.
Scale
0–100 points; 100 is perfect. Enron Complaint J as written: Luna 79.3 (recall 73%, precision 86%); Terra 75.3 (recall 64%, precision 91%); Sol 79.7 (recall 73%, precision 88%); Jev 71.8 (recall 59%, precision 91%).
Why this metric
Recall is the legal obligation, precision is the bill; F1 is the single number that punishes trading one for the other.
Caveat
Pooled over requests, so a large request weighs more than a small one; the gold itself is panel-made for Mallinckrodt and Endo and TREC's for Enron.
