Are the benchmark corpora — and the cases behind them — already inside the models?

A training-data contamination probe for the eDiscovery relevance-review study, in two parts. Part I asks whether the documents and people of six corpora are inside three models (four channels, two calibration anchors). Part II asks whether the legal matters behind the corpora are — the allegations, the players, the record, the outcome — and whether that knowledge is actionable for a relevance call. Run 2026-10-03; 6,056 probe items per model; total API spend $18.16.

Part I — Are the documents and the people inside the model?

Methodology

1. The question

The main study scores TypeSafe's Jev against frontier LLMs on relevance review over six corpora. Four of the six are real and have been public for years: the Enron e-mails (released 2003, part of The Pile), the Jeb Bush e-mails (posted on the web by the Bush campaign in 2015, then distributed by NIST), the Mallinckrodt opioid e-mails (UCSF/JHU archive, 2021–23) and CUAD (an NLP benchmark whose contracts are SEC filings). Veridian, written by us in September 2026, is guaranteed to post-date every model's training data; and the sixth corpus, the Endo opioid e-mails (the same UCSF/JHU archive, but a production published in 2024–26, after the models' cutoffs), is real and should post-date it — a held-out collection from a matter the models can know.

If an LLM has already absorbed a corpus — its documents, the people in it, the benchmark built on it, or even the published relevance judgments — its score on that corpus may not transfer to a client's never-seen collection. Jev, whose vendor states it is a System One classifier not pre-trained on public text, would not enjoy the same help if that claim holds; the claim is itself under test in this repository; the full-document ablation (§20) tested it and found Jev's knowledge effect indistinguishable from zero on Enron at that sample size — consistent with the claim, not a demonstration of it, though round 2 found its matter-topic scores on Jeb Bush do depend on the real names (-3.1 [-5.6, -0.7] on the Jeb Bush matter topics), a small familiarity effect consistent with public-web knowledge of public figures and not by itself evidence of training on the collection — and the classifier-native tests (§21) then probed it through its own interface: The classifier-native tests are consistent with Jev not having been trained on these collections, and they show it knows the cases behind them at effect sizes comparable to the LLMs. Knowing the case did not detectably move its F1 on Enron (+0.0 [-2.6, +2.6]). (classifier-native report; §21) So before leaning on any corpus for a headline claim, we want to know: how much of this does the model already know?

2. What "already knows" could mean: four channels

"Contamination" is not one thing. A model could be advantaged through four distinct routes, and each calls for a different measurement.

The channels are ordered from "plausible and benign-ish" to "fatal". V and E are the ones most likely to be doing real work in a review task; L is the one that would make a result meaningless; B sits between.

3. Design principles

  1. Measure exposure, not effect. This probe asks whether the knowledge is in the model. Whether it helps is a separate experiment — the pseudonymisation ablation, reported in §20 — which this probe told us where to run; Part II (§13–19) takes the intermediate step of asking whether the case, as opposed to the documents, is in the model.
  2. One cheap probe per channel, no document classification. Every item is a short prompt; the whole battery for three models cost $18.16. The study's expensive runs are untouched.
  3. Identical treatment for every corpus. Same prompts, same sampling procedure, same scorer; corpora differ only in the material drawn from them.
  4. Interpret only relative to controls. Free-text scores have no natural zero (see §4), so every number is read as a position between a floor and a ceiling measured the same way.
  5. Same decoding as the study. Each model runs at the study's floor effort setting (reasoning.effort=none) and temperature 0, so this is the configuration whose knowledge we actually care about.

4. Why the controls are the point

Suppose a model continues an Enron e-mail and matches 17% of the original words. Is that memorisation? Not necessarily: e-mails are formulaic, and a model that has never seen the message can still guess "Please let me know if you have any questions" from the register. The raw score is uninterpretable without knowing what guessing alone produces.

The floor. Veridian is the answer to "what does a model score when it can know nothing?" It is a corpus of e-mails, memos, chat logs and calendar invites in the same register as the real corpora, written in 2026. Its score on every probe is the genre-knowledge baseline, and any real corpus has to beat it.

The ceiling. The first version of this plan used Enron as the contaminated anchor: it is in The Pile, so surely a model has memorised it. The first run falsified that — no model reproduced Enron e-mail text — which left the top of the scale undefined. We therefore added explicit positive controls: material that every model has certainly seen many times, chosen so that each channel has one.

5. The probes, item by item

V · Verbatim continuation

60 documents per corpus (20 for the Titanic CSV). The prompt shows the headers and the first ~45% of the body and asks for "the next 60 words, exactly as in the original if you recognise it, your best guess otherwise". Two pitfalls are handled at construction: the body is cut before the first quoted reply or forwarded header, because a reply chain is guessable from the headers; and any run of words that already appears in the prompt (digits masked) earns no credit (the novel flag), because a model can copy a repeated template without remembering anything. A third is handled by a two-stage quality filter on the windows themselves — rules for boilerplate and degraded text, then a small-model screen that keeps only original, custodian-authored text and rejects public text reproduced inside the corpus and low-information windows (details and counts in §8). Scores: word-level LCS-F1 against the true continuation; the longest run of consecutive matching words; the share of documents with a run of ≥ 8 and ≥ 15 words. A refusal ("I can’t provide the continuation of a private e-mail…") is detected when the response opens with one and names what it withholds; refusals are excluded from every V statistic, for the real corpora and the floor alike, and reported as a separate rate (§8).

E1 · Entity free recall

30 names per corpus, extracted from the From/To/Cc headers and tiered by how many documents they appear in: the 10 most frequent, 10 from ranks 11–40, and 10 from the tail (1–2 documents). The split matters: Ken Lay is world knowledge, a tail-tier correspondent is only knowable from the mailbox. Prompt: "Who is {name}, active around {era}? Name their organisation and role, or answer UNKNOWN." A hit is an answer that names the corpus's organisation. Veridian's cast is fictional, so a hit is impossible and any non-UNKNOWN answer is a confabulation. (For the canon anchor the cast is fixed and tiered by fame instead.)

E2 · Entity recognition matrix

Free recall is a strict test; people often recognise what they cannot recall, and so do models. Each name is therefore also asked, yes/no, against its own organisation and each of the other five (Enron, the Bush administration, Mallinckrodt/Covidien, Endo Pharmaceuticals, Veridian Orthopedics, the framing of the Constitution). Hit rate is yes to the true organisation; the false-alarm rate on foils is the model's acquiescence baseline; d′ combines them. Asking pairwise rather than multiple-choice prevents answering by elimination ("never heard of him → must be the fictional company").

B · Benchmark knowledge

Seven free-text questions: list the TREC 2016 athome4 topics; describe TREC Legal 2010 topics 301–304 and 2009 topics 201–207; list CUAD's clause categories; describe the Mallinckrodt collection in the Opioid Industry Documents Archive; describe the (fictional) Veridian ApexHip matter; list the 20 Newsgroups. Graded by the fraction of true titles recovered, and for Veridian by whether the model confabulated or said it did not know.

L · Label recall from the id alone

100 (document, topic) pairs per corpus with no document text: "According to the published judgments, was document {id} relevant to topic {t}?" The pairs are balanced 50/50 within each topic, so that the topic named in the prompt carries no label information and chance is exactly 50%. This was not the original design: the first build balanced across the whole corpus, and the largest model scored 70% on Mallinckrodt — a corpus whose labels were never published — simply by saying "relevant" to every broad issue and "not relevant" to every narrow one. The report now prints the best any topic-only rule could score on the sample beside each accuracy. For Titanic the same logic balances within the passenger's title (Mr/Mrs/Miss/Master), since the title reveals sex and age, which predict survival.

Results

Overview · documents, case and overall scores per dataset and model

Before the probe-by-probe results, the whole study on one grid. Each number is a 0–100 contamination score: the dataset's position between the fictional floor (0) and the saturated ceilings (100). Under each model there are three — the documents score (V, E, L, B), the case score (M0, M1, M2) and the overall mean — because the two kinds of knowledge diverge: Enron is near the floor on its documents and near the ceiling on its case. For the largest model the order is CUAD contracts → Enron e-mails → Jeb Bush e-mails → Endo opioid e-mails → Mallinckrodt e-mails → Big Thorium → Veridian; the two top datasets get there for different reasons — CUAD scores on its documents, Enron on its case. In one sentence: the evidence points to matter-level exposure being common and document-level exposure being rare for internal e-mail, and we found at most a 2.6-point consequence of the former for the LLMs' review accuracy on three matters (Enron, Jeb Bush and CUAD; §20).

Endo, the post-cutoff corpus. The sixth corpus was added for one reason: it is a real collection published after the models stopped learning, from a matter they can know. Its publication date (2024–26) is what establishes that the documents were not in training; the probes are the check, and they show no document-level signal, as expected. It gives the study's clearest "known case, post-cutoff documents" profile. On every document channel it sits at the Veridian floor — verbatim LCS-F1 0.14–0.16 against Veridian's 0.15–0.17, free recall of its cast 0/30 for every model, recognition 0–3 yes of 30, labels at chance (47% / 52% / 51%) — while the case behind it is known: identified from a nameless sketch by GPT-5.6 Luna, GPT-5.6 Terra, GPT-5.6 Sol, 55%–70% of the fact checklist recalled (better than Mallinckrodt's 43%–52%), 2 / 6 / 6 discriminative named terms. Composite documents·case·overall: 2·64·33 / 3·59·31 / 1·62·32 (GPT-5.6 Luna / GPT-5.6 Terra / GPT-5.6 Sol). Endo is the Enron pattern from the other side — a matter in the model, documents that are not — on a collection where the date, not the probe, supplies the proof; it is consistent with the unit of contamination being the matter rather than the document. It is the recommended held-out benchmark going forward. One caveat on its gold rather than its profile: Endo's relevance labels come from a three-OpenAI-model panel (the Anthropic and Gemini keys were absent), unlike Mallinckrodt's mixed panel. 8. V · Verbatim memorisation

V · Verbatim: longest exact run per document (words, of 60). Dots are documents; the bar is the mean; dashed line = 15 words.

The strip plot is the most honest view of verbatim memorisation: it is sparse and bimodal, not a gentle mean. On the canon anchor the large model reproduces 56 of 60 words on average and 98% of essays have a ≥ 15-word exact run; on the Titanic CSV every chunk is reproduced down to ticket numbers and fares. On the e-mail corpora almost every document sits at 2–4 words, which is what two people writing English will share by accident.

Chaff is not memorisation. A window whose true continuation is a confidentiality disclaimer, a signature block or a forwarded-message header tests genre knowledge, not training data: every model writes "This e-mail and any attachments are confidential…" from scratch. A window whose continuation is a Federal Register notice, a wire story or a newsletter forwarded into the mailbox tests memorisation of the public web, not of the collection (the 34-word Mallinckrodt run that was the e-mail corpora's longest is a DEA notice from the Federal Register). And a window whose opening is header-only or garbled, or whose continuation is a table, a list or a repeat, cannot identify the document even to a model that memorised it. Such windows are removed in two stages — rules (contam/boilerplate.py) and a GPT-5.6 Luna screen (contam/screen.py) that keeps only original internal text — and the builder replaces them from the same deterministic order, so the quota per corpus is met with clean windows. Every V number on this page, including the strip plot and the composite, is computed on the pool; excluded windows stay in the file, are scored for the record and are flagged per item in scored_items.jsonl. The Founding documents and Titanic CSV anchors are public text by design and are exempt from the public-text class. Pool per corpus: Enron e-mails 60/85 kept (public text 15, boilerplate rules 10), 25 replacements; Jeb Bush e-mails 60/77 kept (boilerplate rules 13, public text 4), 17 replacements; Mallinckrodt e-mails 60/99 kept (boilerplate rules 27, public text 12), 39 replacements; Endo opioid e-mails 60/97 kept (boilerplate rules 28, public text 9), 37 replacements; CUAD contracts 60/73 kept (boilerplate rules 7, public text 5, low information 1), 13 replacements; Veridian 60/61 kept (public text 1), 1 replacements; Founding documents 60/60 kept; Titanic CSV 13/20 kept (low information 7).

Refusals are a policy reading, not a memorisation reading. A response that opens "I can’t provide the continuation of a private e-mail" tells us what the model will do with someone else's correspondence, not whether it has seen it, so every number in this section — LCS-F1, runs, the strip plot and the Mann-Whitney test against the floor — is computed over the answered items only, for the real corpora and for Veridian alike, with the refusal rate shown beside it (the refusals-as-zero mean is kept in the summary as lcs_f_mean_all). The detector requires the response to open with a first-person refusal and to name what is withheld within its first 30 words; an e-mail that happens to continue "I apologize for missing the call" is not a refusal. GPT-5.6 Luna refused 29 of 481 pool items (20 citing private or non-user-provided text, 5 giving no reason, 4 citing copyright) and the rate is sharply corpus-dependent: Veridian 25%, Big Thorium 2%, Jeb Bush e-mails 2%, Mallinckrodt e-mails 3%, Endo opioid e-mails 15%, CUAD contracts 2%; none on Enron e-mails, Founding documents, Titanic CSV. The fictional floor is refused most of all — modern-looking corporate e-mail about a product-liability matter reads as private — so scoring refusals as zero would have pushed its LCS-F1 from 0.152 down to 0.114 and the e-mail corpora (Enron e-mails / Jeb Bush e-mails / Mallinckrodt e-mails / Endo opioid e-mails) to p = 0.001 / 0.016 / 0.018 / 0.051 against it; on answered items they are p = 0.16 / 0.53 / 0.47 / 0.17. That the 2001 Enron mail is never refused while the 2010s Endo and Veridian mail is suggests the policy keys on how recent and how corporate the text looks, not on its source. GPT-5.6 Terra and GPT-5.6 Sol refused nothing at effort=none.

With the pool filtered, the e-mail corpora have almost no long runs left to inspect: 5 windows across the four corpora reach an exact run of ≥ 8 words for any model (longest 10; 4 with a novel run of ≥ 8). The long runs the earlier pool contained were all chaff and are now excluded with their reasons recorded: a 60-word repeated disk-space error message in Enron (degenerate repetition), a list of internet-famous one-liners (public text), a Federal Register / DEA notice quoted inside a Mallinckrodt e-mail (34 words — public text, memorised from its source and not from the mailbox), a weekly sales-report template whose bullets repeat from the prompt (repetition), and an automated expense-report notification (auto-message). CUAD is different in kind: 10% of pool excerpts have ≥ 15-word runs for the large model after standard clauses and mirrored provisions are removed, including a SpinCo/RemainCo licence reproduced with its party-specific defined terms intact. Those are not boilerplate.

Part II — Is the case inside the model?

Methodology

13. Why the case matters: the legal frame

Part I treated the corpora as text: are the documents memorised, are the correspondents known, are the benchmark's topics and labels known? It found the e-mail corpora near the floor on documents and the cast known only where it is famous. That is reassuring, and it is also the wrong unit of analysis for legal discovery.

A relevance review is not defined by a corpus. It is defined by a matter: a complaint, the allegations in it, the parties and their people, the factual record the parties will fight over, and — once it is over — the outcome. A reviewer who already knows the case reads every document differently from one who does not. They know which transactions were later found to be fraudulent, which executives were indicted, which code names mattered, which customers were the "pill mills", which year the shredding happened. They can tell that an e-mail from the CFO about an offshore vehicle is responsive to a request about debt-concealment without the request saying "offshore vehicle". The request text and the task context we give every model are the same; the prior each model brings to them is not.

Four of the study's six corpora come from matters that are among the most-documented in American legal history. Enron is the textbook corporate fraud; the Mallinckrodt and Endo documents come from the opioid MDL, the largest civil litigation of its era; the Jeb Bush e-mails are the public record of a two-term governorship whose controversies (the 2000 recount, Terri Schiavo) were national news. Jev has read none of this. If a frontier model has, then on these corpora it is not a fresh reviewer but a reviewer who has read the press, the indictments and the settlements — and its score is partly a score for hindsight.

So Part II asks a different question from Part I: not "has the model seen these documents?" but "does the model know this case, and is what it knows the kind of knowledge that changes a relevance call?" Four things a case-aware reviewer has, each of which becomes a probe:

14. The matter probes and their controls

M0 · Matter identification

For Enron we use the TREC Legal Track's own pleadings, which were written to be fictional: Complaint J puts Enron's exact class period (June 1, 1999 – December 2, 2001), prepay transactions, FAS 140, shredding and energy-schedule manipulation under the name "Volteron Corp."; Complaint K is an oil-spill class action over the sinking of the "Bleak Horizon". The model sees ~2,500 characters of each complaint and is asked which real company or case it is modelled on. For Mallinckrodt, Endo, Jeb Bush, Veridian and the ceiling matter we write a short sketch with every proper noun removed. A hit names the real matter. For Veridian the correct answer is "no real matter"; we record whether the model instead names a real template (DePuy ASR, Zimmer Biomet, Stryker…), because a synthetic matter built on a famous archetype inherits some of that archetype's knowledge.

M1 · Matter recall against a checklist

"Describe {matter}: parties, key allegations, key people, main events with dates, outcome." Graded against a hand-written checklist of 16–20 facts per matter spanning five categories (parties, allegations, people, events, outcome), keyword-matched on whole words. Two refinements make the number meaningful for the study. First, every item is flagged as in context or beyond context: the study's task context already tells every model that Mallinckrodt is accused of weak suspicious-order monitoring, so reciting that is not an advantage; knowing about the 2017 DEA settlement or the Chapter 11 is. We report both the total and the beyond-context share. Second, the Veridian matter is asked under its full fictional caption (MDL No. 3102, N.D. Ind.); the right answer is "I do not know this matter", and anything else is a confabulation whose length we record.

M2 · Evidence prior

The model is given exactly what it gets in the study — the matter context and one request — and asked, as the review lead with no documents yet seen, for the 25 most specific things it expects in responsive documents, each typed as a person, organisation, code name, product, place, period or keyword. Scoring is against the labelled corpus. Terms already present in the context or request are discarded (the model was told those). Of the remaining named terms we ask two questions: is it grounded — does it actually occur in at least two judged documents? — and is it discriminative — is it at least twice as frequent among responsive documents as overall (lift ≥ 2, in ≥ 2 responsive documents)? A grounded, discriminative named term is a piece of case knowledge that a search or a classifier could use directly. Generic keywords ("spreadsheet", "wholesaler") are scored the same way but reported separately: they measure vocabulary, not knowledge of the case.

Enron supplies two built-in contrasts. The seven Complaint J requests (201–207) are the real scandal — prepay, FAS 140, forecasts, shredding, energy schedules, analysts — plus one control topic, fantasy football, that TREC added for exactly this reason; the three Complaint K requests (301–303: oil and gas drilling, spill response, lobbying) are not Enron's story at all. And the Complaint J requests are run twice: once as TREC wrote them, under the "Volteron" pseudonym, and once with the company named. The difference between those two runs is the knowledge that the pseudonym suppressed, and the difference between J and K is the knowledge that is specific to the actual case.

M3 · Metadata-only relevance

For each request we draw a balanced sample of responsive and non-responsive e-mails (20 + 20; 15 + 15 for Jeb Bush) and show the model the matter context, the request and only the header metadata — no body. Each e-mail is shown twice: with Date, From, To, Cc and Subject, and with Date and Subject alone. The paired difference in accuracy is what seeing the people added. Because the sample is balanced within every request, chance is 50% and the request itself carries no label information; a lexical baseline (a request-title word appearing in the subject line) shows what plain keyword matching achieves on the same e-mails. Veridian is the control for the people channel: its names are fictional, so any lift its headers give comes from structure (domains, distribution lists, FDA addresses), not from knowing who anyone is. For Enron the context states that "Volteron" is Enron, so that the headers condition does not also reveal which company it is.

The ladder · real matters at graded exposure

Two anchors fix the ends of a scale; they do not show its shape. To read the study's matters as positions rather than as isolated numbers, M0 and M1 are also run on a ladder of 24 real matters from the last ninety years, chosen in families that mirror the corpora: accounting and securities frauds for Enron (WorldCom, HealthSouth, Peregrine Systems, Equity Funding, McKesson & Robbins); opioid cases for Mallinckrodt and Endo (Purdue, Insys, Rochester Drug Co-operative, Teva, JUUL as the adjacent public-nuisance case); e-mail in public life for Jeb Bush (the Clinton server, Bridgegate, the Sony Pictures hack); device mass torts for Veridian (Dalkon Shield, DePuy ASR, the 3M earplugs, Bair Hugger); landmark disputes across the century (Bhopal, Texaco v. Pennzoil, Dieselgate, Theranos, FTX); and two post-cutoff controls — SEC accounting-fraud actions filed in 2026 against the former officers of Near Intelligence and against Meyer Global Management — which are real but cannot be in the training data. Each rung gets the same de-identified sketch and the same checklist grading as the study's matters (10–18 facts each). Alongside, an independent proxy for public exposure is recorded for every matter: the length, number of language editions and last-twelve-months pageviews of its English Wikipedia article (none for the fictional and post-cutoff matters, and for a few real ones that have no article). The expectation is a gradient — recall rising with footprint within each family — with the post-cutoff matters behaving like Veridian.

17. The ladder: where the study's matters sit among real cases

GPT-5.6 Luna (small)GPT-5.6 Terra (mid)GPT-5.6 Sol (large)

The anchors fixed the ends of the scale; the ladder shows its shape, on matters chosen to resemble the study's own. Three things stand out. First, the floor for a real, litigated matter is high. The lowest real rung for each model is GPT-5.6 Luna: Mallinckrodt opioid litigation at 39%, GPT-5.6 Terra: Peregrine Systems at 33%, GPT-5.6 Sol: Mallinckrodt opioid litigation at 44%; nothing that was ever charged, tried or settled in public comes out blank. A distributor criminally charged in 2019 that has no Wikipedia article at all, and the largest MDL in history, which also has none, are recalled at Rochester Drug Co-operative 40% / 80% / 90%; 3M Combat Arms earplugs 75% / 100% / 100%. Legal matters live in sources — DOJ and SEC press releases, dockets, opinions, law-firm client alerts — that are crawled thoroughly and read rarely, so public popularity understates what the models carry.

Second, scale flattens the ladder. Across the matters with an article, the rank correlation between twelve-month pageviews and recall is GPT-5.6 Luna +0.48 (p = 0.015), GPT-5.6 Terra +0.48 (p = 0.014), GPT-5.6 Sol +0.25 (not significant, p = 0.22). For the small and mid models, fame predicts knowledge: a 1938 wholesaler fraud or a 1973 insurance fraud is half-known, a 2002 fraud that made the business pages for a season is two-thirds known, a byword is fully known. For GPT-5.6 Sol the correlation is not significant at 27 rungs — Texaco v. Pennzoil, HealthSouth, Dalkon Shield and Rochester Drug all score ≥ 90% — consistent with it knowing the obscure rungs about as well as the famous ones, though 27 matters is a small sample for the comparison. The practical consequence for the study is that "obscure" is not a defence at frontier scale; only "after the cutoff" is.

Third, identification is nearly binary. 26 of the 27 real matters are named by every model from a sketch with every proper noun removed; Enron is missed only by GPT-5.6 Sol. Recognition of a fact pattern precedes recall of its details and is almost complete for anything that reached the national press, however long ago. A pseudonymised complaint or a redacted request is identifying information for any real matter the model has met at all; §15 showed that for Enron, and the ladder shows it is the rule.

The post-cutoff rungs are the control the fictional matter cannot supply: real cases the models cannot have read about. For the September 2026 adviser case, GPT-5.6 Luna said it could not tell, GPT-5.6 Terra named a different case, GPT-5.6 Sol said it could not tell; asked to describe the case by name, all three decline (0% / 0% / 0% of the facts the question did not state). Near Intelligence straddles the cutoff and behaves accordingly: GPT-5.6 Luna named it, GPT-5.6 Terra named it, GPT-5.6 Sol named it; recall beyond the question 22% / 0% / 0%. One caveat: the sketches carry their dates, and the models use "2026 is in the future" as a reason to decline. A stricter version would omit the date and see whether they still know they do not know.

Where the study's matters sit. Enron (84% / 79% / 90%) is in the top group with Bhopal, the Clinton server, Microsoft and WorldCom — the matters the models know almost entirely. Jeb Bush (50% / 44% / 67%) is well below the two e-mail scandals that share its family (Bridgegate 92% / 92% / 100%, the Clinton server 93% / 87% / 100%): a governorship is remembered as a list of controversies, not as a cast. Mallinckrodt (39% / 33% / 44%) is the least-known real matter on the whole ladder for every model — below Rochester Drug and Insys, its opioid-family peers, and below a 1938 fraud. That is the useful surprise: of the real e-mail corpora, the one whose matter the models know least is a recent one, because its record is an MDL docket and a bankruptcy rather than a trial with a narrative. Endo (62% / 44% / 62%) sits just above it: the matter is known at least as well as Mallinckrodt's by every model despite a smaller public footprint (13,340 pageviews against 36,785), while its documents, published after the cutoff, are at the floor — the ladder shows the case and Part I shows the collection, and they disagree in the direction the study wants. Asked afterwards which company the renamed Enron documents came from — production headers stripped, ticker mapped — the models still named Enron in 61% / 82% / 94% of a 200-document sample (GPT-5.6 Luna / GPT-5.6 Terra / GPT-5.6 Sol), and in 48% / 77% / 94% of the 77 documents that contained no knowledge-bearing name at all. They recognise the case from the fact pattern (the deals, the dates, the business), not from the names. The ablation therefore removes only the name channel: Δ is a lower bound on the effect of case knowledge, and the evidence is that the names are not the main channel through which it would act. A clean effect test on Enron would need the fact pattern rewritten, which for a real matter is impossible without destroying the documents; the way forward is a corpus the models have not seen.

Jev on FAS 140. One request moves against the grain. Mallinckrodt, with and without the case brief. Generalisation checks: four ways the result could have failed

Four checks on the claim on the matters tested, knowing the case changed review accuracy by at most a few F1 points, each chosen so that it could have undermined it; each is read in claim language and Jev is a system under test throughout. Full tables and figures in the verification report. New OpenAI spend $10.95 of a $15 cap.

  • A · Knowledge-dependence (Enron J). Only 6.9% [5.4, 8.9] of the Enron J documents are knowledge-dependent (relevance visible only with outside Enron knowledge; FAS 140 25%, every other request ≤ 8%). Contamination predicts higher named accuracy on them and a larger renaming drop on them. Neither holds: every system is less accurate on them when named (61% / 61% / 64% / 66% vs 83% / 81% / 83% / 78%; Luna / Terra / Sol / Jev), and the most accurate system on them is Jev. Renaming on the knowledge-dependent subset (n = 59) moved F1 by Luna +4.1 [-6.7, +14.9]; Terra +4.0 [-17.6, +26.2]; Sol +17.3 [+4.8, +32.1]; Jev -15.7 [-33.3, -0.7] — renaming helped GPT-5.6 Sol; the one drop whose interval excludes zero is Jev's. The LLMs' Enron J edge over Jev sits on the self-contained documents. Strengthens the claim: where knowledge could help, no system behaves as if it did.
  • B · Ranking stability. System order is not stable across corpora: Kendall W = 0.14 over 5 corpora × 4 systems (p ≈ 0.56; four objects give W almost no power). Within each LLM, F1 does not trend with its own case-knowledge score (Spearman ρ Luna +0.0, Terra -0.2, Sol -0.2, n = 4). The mean-LLM − Jev F1 gap grows monotonically with the LLMs' case knowledge: Enron J (case 84) +6.3 → Mallinckrodt (case 53) -2.0 → Veridian (case 0) -4.1 pp. That is the pattern contamination predicts, but it is also what corpus type predicts (real human-judged e-mail at 49% prevalence on Enron J against LLM-panel and synthetic-planner gold at 11% and 10% on Mallinckrodt and Veridian), and checks A and D test the mechanism directly on both ends of the gradient and find none. Ambiguous, leaning against the claim on its own; unresolved at n = 4.
  • C · Counterfactual conflicts. On 30 short documents per matter whose text contradicts a well-known fact (Roxicodone as an antacid; the Schiavo file as a road renaming), the call follows world knowledge rather than the text on 8–17% of pairs pooled over the four real matters (Luna 17% / Terra 14% / Sol 12% / Jev 8%; every system reads the factual versions at ≥ 99%), almost entirely in one direction — a well-known responsive token keeps a document responsive — and concentrated on Jeb Bush, Mallinckrodt and Endo, near zero on Enron. A header plus one sentence naming Raptor, LJM2 or Chewco is called responsive to the FAS 140 request 0% of the time with the criteria as written (fictional twin 0%; mean p 0.11 vs 0.08), and 78% vs 0% only once the criteria name the vehicles (83% vs 0% with the ablation's 'The Company is Enron Corp.' context added). The ablation's FAS 140 loss is therefore not a bare-name reflex: it is Jev reading request and document together, and using what the names mean when the request points at them.
  • T2 · minimal-edit label flip. On documents edited to flip their true relevance, Jev stays with the old published label on 20% [13%, 29%] of Enron edits (LLMs 27%–31%); 30% [22%, 40%] of Jeb Bush edits (LLMs 32%–36%), against 46% on Veridian, whose labels were never published (LLMs 41%–50%). Memorised labels would put the public collections above Veridian; none does (Jev minus Veridian: Enron -26.2 [-38.8, -13.4]; Jeb Bush -15.7 [-29.8, -1.6]). Consistent with no label memorisation; caveat: the second-model check confirms the intended flip on only 4–7 of 10 sampled edits per collection, which dilutes label-following toward the old label for every system equally.
  • T3 · paraphrase sensitivity (Jev only; it returns a probability). Paraphrasing a document (headers kept) moves Jev's probability by a mean |Δp| of Enron 0.006 [0.004, 0.007], calls flipped 0.7%; Endo 0.012 [0.010, 0.014], calls flipped 0.9%; Veridian 0.006 [0.005, 0.008], calls flipped 0.4%. Enron — the corpus in public pre-training text — is indistinguishable from the synthetic floor (Mann–Whitney p = 0.75); the one corpus that differs is Endo (p < 0.001), which post-dates the cutoff and cannot have been memorised. Consistent with no document memorisation.
  • T4 · published vs unpublished labels. A system that had memorised the published judgments would agree with them more than with an LLM panel on judged documents; Jev does the reverse. Enron: agreement with the human qrels 74% on judged documents, with the LLM panel 92% on the same documents (panel vs qrels 78%); judged − unjudged agreement with the panel +23.2 pp on panel-positives (n 49 vs 8) and -4.5 pp on panel-negatives. Jeb Bush: 76% with the qrels, 86% with the panel (panel vs qrels 86%); judged − unjudged +10.6 [-11.2, +33.7] on panel-positives (n 1,174 vs 14) and -0.5 [-1.4, +0.1] on panel-negatives. The positive-side differences are wide and rest on a handful of unjudged positives, the negative side shows no judged-set advantage: consistent with no label memorisation. Judged documents were pool-selected by the TREC participants' systems and are not a random sample of the collection; that confound is the binding limit on this test's power.

Reading. The classifier-native tests are consistent with Jev not having been trained on these collections, and they show it knows the cases behind them at effect sizes comparable to the LLMs. Knowing the case did not detectably move its F1 on Enron (+0.0 [-2.6, +2.6]). T1 is knowledge of what the names mean — drug brands, Enron's vehicles, Florida controversies — not memory of any document: it is inconsistent with a reading of the vendor's statement under which the classifier has no real-world knowledge, and consistent with either pre-training on public text or synthetic training data distilled from a model that has that knowledge; these tests cannot separate the two. T2–T4 bound memorisation of the documents and labels only at these sample sizes and for these collections. Jev remains a system under test and the vendor's statement remains a claim.

23. Caveats

  • Three models, one vendor. The Anthropic and Gemini arms of the study have not been probed; the runner will fill them in when keys are available. The within-vendor scale gradient suggests the pattern will hold, but that is an expectation, not a measurement.
  • Embedded public text (government notices, licence banners, jokes) is memorised from its public source and the verbatim probe cannot tell that apart from corpus memorisation. The top runs per corpus were inspected by hand; a cross-check against a web index would automate it.
  • CUAD's floor is not genre-matched. Veridian is e-mail; legal drafting is more predictable than e-mail, so part of CUAD's gap above the floor may be genre rather than memorisation even after standard clauses and mirrored provisions are filtered out of the pool. The long party-specific runs are the number that cannot be explained that way. A post-cutoff EDGAR contract sample is the control that would separate the two, and is the next addition to the battery.
  • The document-level null is absence of evidence, from 60 windows per corpus, 60 words each, at temperature 0, with one prompt form. It is informative because the same probe fires on the canon anchor and on CUAD, and it is bounded: a model could hold a few documents of a 46,000-document mailbox verbatim without a 60-window sample finding them. The Enron corpus is in open training sets (The Pile), so presence in training data is likely; what is not detectable is document-level memory, which in the literature tracks repetition rather than presence.
  • Refusals. GPT-5.6 Luna declines to continue 25% of Veridian's and 15% of Endo's e-mails as private, and 0% of Enron's; the other models 0%. Refusals are excluded from every V statistic on both sides; scored as zeros they had masked the floor for Luna, and the earlier reading of a small-model residual above the floor as a genre effect is withdrawn.
  • The pool filter is a judgement. The rules are deterministic; the screen is a small model at temperature 0 whose verdicts are cached and inspectable (results/contam/v_screen.jsonl). It is lenient rather than strict — a few predictable continuations survive it — and the novel flag and the per-item review page are the backstop.
  • Jeb Bush text is available locally only for the 600-document subset, so its name tiers are shallow (the top tier starts at 14 documents) and its verbatim sample is drawn from a narrower pool than the others.
  • Name collisions. A common name may belong to a better-known person (a Veridian tail name collided with a real analyst). Grading on the target organisation makes collisions a miss, which is conservative.
  • Benchmark grading is keyword-based and coarse; one of Sol's seven TREC 2009 guesses ("204 — document destruction") matched by what is probably coincidence.
  • Exposure is mostly not effect. Parts I and the first three matter probes measure what is in the model; M3 and the ablation (§20) measure a change in a relevance call. The round-1 ablation removes only the names, and the models still recognise the case from the fact pattern in most renamed documents, so its small Δ is a lower bound on the effect of case knowledge, not the whole of it; round 2 adds paraphrase (which removes the memorised text) and the brief-injection check adds the knowledge instead of removing it, and neither moved the LLMs' F1 beyond noise.
  • Jev's familiarity effect is small and is not a contamination verdict. Its matter-topic scores depend on the real names (-3.1 [-5.6, -0.7] on the Jeb Bush matter topics; FAS 140 on Enron; the knowledge-dependent Enron documents), which is what an encoder with public-web knowledge of public figures would show; it is not by itself evidence of training on the collections or their labels, and T2–T4 and the CUAD paraphrase find no sign of either. For evaluations: a Jev score on a famous-figure collection may run a few points high relative to an unknown matter.
  • The CUAD paraphrase control is not genre-matched. The cost of paraphrase was measured on Veridian e-mail, not on unseen contracts; a post-cutoff EDGAR sample with expert clause labels is the clean control and was not affordable. The within-CUAD dose–response (no growth of Δ with the contract's memorisation score) is the primary control.
  • System ordering is not stable across corpora, and the LLM − Jev gap widens with the LLMs' case knowledge. Kendall W = 0.14 over the four systems on 5 corpora; the mean-LLM − Jev F1 gap runs Enron J (case 84) +6.3 → Mallinckrodt (case 53) -2.0 → Veridian (case 0) -4.1 pp. Contamination predicts this ordering, but so does corpus type (real human-judged e-mail at 49% prevalence on Enron J against LLM-panel and synthetic-planner gold at 11% and 10% on Mallinckrodt and Veridian); with four systems and three kinds of gold the two cannot be separated, and the checks that test the mechanism directly (knowledge-dependence, brief injection) find none. It is reported as the one check that did not come out clean, and it is unresolved at n = 4.
  • Matter rubrics are hand-written and keyword-graded. Each checklist has 16–20 items chosen to span parties, allegations, people, events and outcome; a model can know a fact and phrase it in a way the keywords miss, so recall shares are lower bounds. The 'beyond context' split depends on what the study's task context happens to state.
  • The Jeb Bush evidence prior and metadata probes run on the 600-document local subset (the collection is not redistributable), so their grounding counts are small and their per-request samples are 15 + 15.
  • Lift is estimated on judged documents, whose sampling (TREC's stratified draws for Enron) differs from the collection; a term's lift is relative to the judged set, not to the whole mailbox.
  • The ladder's footprint proxy is rough. Wikipedia pageviews measure public attention, not what is crawlable: two rungs with no article at all are recalled at 80–100% by the mid and large models, and for five matters the lookup falls back to a company or biography page broader than the case (flagged in the table). The rank correlation it supports (≈ +0.5 for the smaller models, ≈ +0.2 for the largest) should be read as "fame is a usable prior for small models", not as a measurement of exposure.
  • The post-cutoff sketches carry their dates. The models decline the 2026 matter partly because the sketch says 2026; a date-free version would test whether they know they do not know. The mid model's confident mis-identification of that matter is a reminder that "named a case" is not "named the right case" — M0 is graded against answer keys, and a wrong confident answer is a hollow mark, not a hit.
  • Ladder rubrics are shorter (10–18 facts) and were written from the public record by the author, not drawn from the study's task contexts; a few items are strict (Equity Funding's reinsurance mechanism, Bhopal's Sevin) and the shares are lower bounds as elsewhere.
  • Veridian's template. The synthetic matter was written to resemble real metal-on-metal hip litigation, and the models say so (§15). Its documents and cast are novel; its archetype is not. That is the right floor for 'knows the documents' and a slightly generous floor for 'knows the kind of case'.
  • Pre-training and distillation are not separable. The code-name swap shows the decision model uses real-world matter knowledge; a base model pre-trained on public text and a model trained on synthetic data generated by a knowledgeable LLM would both show it. No test here distinguishes them.
  • The minimal-edit instrument is noisy. The second-model check confirms the intended flip on only 4–7 of 10 sampled edits per collection, which dilutes label-following toward the old label for every system equally; the within-collection comparison of Jev with the LLMs stands, the contrast with Veridian is the weaker leg.
  • The published-vs-unpublished test has little power on the positive side. Relevant documents in these collections were almost all judged (pool selection), so the unjudged side has a handful of panel-positives and the judged − unjudged difference on them is wide; the negative side is the informative cell, and it shows no judged-set advantage.

24. What this decides

  1. Weight the study's conclusions toward the e-mail corpora and Veridian; report CUAD with the two-channel contamination caveat.
  2. Treat Enron as a known case, not an unknown mailbox. We found no document-level memory (Part I), but the matter is in the model (Part II): the pseudonymised complaint is identified by two of three models (the third names Reliant, a company in the same scandal), the record is recited 85% / 80% / 90%, and naming the company unlocks real, discriminative names. Any Enron result should be reported as "on a matter the model knows".
  3. Report the effect as measured, with its bound — now on three matters. For the LLMs, knowing the case changed review accuracy by at most a few F1 points on Enron, Jeb Bush and CUAD under three manipulations (remove the names, remove the memorised text by paraphrase, inject the case through a brief): no knowledge effect beyond 2.6 F1 points, intervals including zero except GPT-5.6 Terra on Jeb Bush (-2.3 [-4.3, -0.3]); on CUAD, where the documents are memorised, paraphrasing away the memorised surface changed F1 by ≤ 1.3 points and no more on the best-remembered contracts. The knowledge-dependent documents (7% of Enron J) are where help would show, and every system does worse there; supplying the case through a brief on Veridian moved F1 by -0.3 / -0.6 / -1.0 / +0.5 (GPT-5.6 Luna / GPT-5.6 Terra / GPT-5.6 Sol / Jev). That still bounds rather than excludes: smaller effects and knowledge that survives every manipulation remain possible, the Enron renaming is a floor because the case is recognised from the fact pattern, and the clean test remains a post-cutoff real matter held out and re-dated as models update. Jev knows the matters, shows no sign of the documents or labels, but its matter-topic scores depend on the real names (-3.1 [-5.6, -0.7] net on Jeb Bush; -18.2 on the FAS 140 request when Enron's vehicles were renamed) — a small familiarity effect of ~2–3 F1 points, consistent with public-web knowledge of public figures and not by itself evidence of training on the collections.
  4. The one anomaly, followed up. Jev lost 18 points of F1 on the FAS 140 request when the Raptor, Talon, LJM2 and Chewco vehicles were renamed, while the LLMs gained. The bare-token check (§21) finds that a header plus one sentence naming a vehicle is called responsive 0% of the time under the request as written (fictional twin 0%) and 78% vs 0% only when the criteria name the vehicles: the loss is not a bare-name reflex but Jev reading request and document together. The code-name swap on four matters then showed the same case knowledge at effect sizes comparable to the LLMs (Enron +17.9, Jeb Bush +34.8, Mallinckrodt +63.2, Endo +10.5 pp). The classifier-native tests are consistent with Jev not having been trained on these collections, and they show it knows the cases behind them at effect sizes comparable to the LLMs. Knowing the case did not detectably move its F1 on Enron (+0.0 [-2.6, +2.6]).
  5. Pseudonymising the complaint is not a defence. TREC's "Bleak Horizon" was decoded by every model and "Volteron" by 2 of 3 from the fact pattern alone (the other named Reliant Energy, a company in the same scandal), and the nameless ladder sketches identified 26 of 28 real matters for every model. If a client corpus is to be used as a clean test, the documents must be renamed, not just the pleading — and even then the fact pattern remains (§20).
  6. Probe the Anthropic and Gemini models with the same battery (≈ $12 more, dominated by the metadata probe) before publishing cross-vendor comparisons.
  7. Adopt the battery for future corpus selection: any candidate corpus and its matter can be placed on these spectra for a few dollars before a single document is classified. The ladder (§17) is the scale to place it on: at frontier scale, assume any matter with a public docket is known in detail, and treat only post-cutoff matters — or synthetic ones — as genuinely unseen.
  8. Build the next clean real corpus from a post-cutoff matter. The two 2026 SEC actions on the ladder behave like Veridian on recall while being real; a document collection from a matter that arose after the models' cutoff (and is re-dated as models update) would give the study a real-world floor to set beside the synthetic one.
