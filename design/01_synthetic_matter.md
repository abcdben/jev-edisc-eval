## Why a medical-device product liability matter

We need a fictional company where 20 distinct-ish RFPs are realistic and where
the same document can plausibly hit several of them. A device maker in an MDL
gives that naturally: engineering, quality/complaints, regulatory (FDA), sales
and surgeon relationships, marketing claims, finance/reserves, board, HR, M&A,
and discovery-about-discovery all touch the same product. It is also far
enough from opioids that the two legs of the experiment stay independent (the
models will have read about the real opioid litigation; they will not have
read about this).

Alternatives if you'd rather: (a) a regional lender in a consumer-finance class
action; (b) a food manufacturer contamination recall; (c) a SaaS company data
breach + customer contract dispute. All support ~20 RFPs but with less
cross-over between them.

## The matter

**In re Veridian Orthopedics ApexHip Products Liability Litigation** (MDL).
Veridian Orthopedics, Inc. (fictional, ~1,800 employees, HQ Warsaw, Indiana)
makes joint implants. Its ApexHip metal-on-metal total hip system, cleared via
510(k) in 2017, is alleged to shed cobalt/chromium wear debris causing tissue
damage and early revision. Plaintiffs allege design defect, failure to warn,
off-label promotion to younger/active patients, and payments to surgeon
opinion leaders. A 2022 lot-specific recall (acetabular cup lots V-2200 to
V-2299, coating adhesion) is a separate, narrower issue. Veridian acquired the
coating technology by buying Kinetic Bearing Technologies in 2016.

Relevant period: 2015-01-01 to 2024-06-30.

## Document population (proposed shape, for discussion)

- **Size**: ~800–1,200 documents. Enough per RFP for stable recall/precision
  (target ≥ 25 true positives per RFP), small enough that the full LLM roster
  is affordable and you could hand-audit gold labels.
- **Per RFP**: positives (clear), gray-area (a reasonable reviewer could go
  either way; we record a gold label *and* a "gray" flag so we can report
  metrics with and without gray docs), and hard negatives (near-miss topic,
  same people, wrong product or wrong scope).
- **Multi-RFP positives**: ~25–35% of positives hit 2+ RFPs by design.
- **Pure noise**: ~30–40% of the corpus is responsive to nothing (HR, IT,
  social, other products, vendor spam, newsletters), which is what a real
  custodian mailbox looks like.
- **Types**: email threads (with quoted history, forwards, CC sprawl), Teams
  chats, memos, meeting minutes, slide outlines, spreadsheet summaries,
  complaint forms, FDA letters, contracts, calendar invites, HR forms, hotline
  intake forms, text messages. Realistic length variation: 2-line chats to
  4-page memos.
- **Realism controls**: consistent cast, dates, and jargon; typos and informal
  tone in chats; internal acronyms defined nowhere; threads where the
  responsive content is buried in quoted history.
- **Gold labels**: assigned at generation time (each doc is authored *to* a
  label set), then a human pass on a sample, and every gray doc gets a
  one-line rationale so disagreements can be adjudicated.
