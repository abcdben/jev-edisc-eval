Case brief (the review protocol's case summary, as supplied to reviewers):

Matter overview:
This matter involves an internal and external legal investigation into alleged bribery by BigThorium, a sustainable energy company. BigThorium responded to a Request for Proposal (RFP) issued by the City of Atlantis for a municipal energy project. The project’s goals included improving energy efficiency and reducing the city’s carbon footprint. BigThorium ultimately won the contract, making the procurement process a central focus of scrutiny. Allegations have arisen that BigThorium employees bribed City of Atlantis officials to secure the award. The investigation examines potential violations of anti-bribery and corruption laws, including state anti-bribery statutes and concepts similar to the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA). Key factual questions include whether improper payments, gifts, or luxury goods were offered or provided to public officials. The review also looks at whether the bidding process was manipulated or unfairly influenced. Third-party intermediaries or consultants potentially used to channel improper benefits are within scope. Internal BigThorium communications about gifts, incentives, or unethical practices are critical. The review is being conducted under the direction of BigThorium’s General Counsel, Evelyn Archer, and outside counsel Daniel Carter of Law Firm X. The protocol governs how reviewers code documents for responsiveness, key status, issues, and privilege. The matter is at the investigation stage, focused on fact development and risk assessment rather than a specific filed lawsuit.

additionalContext:
The review covers emails, internal documents, text messages, and transactional records related to the City of Atlantis RFP and municipal energy project. Reviewers must code documents as Responsive/Not Responsive, mark Key Documents, apply issue tags from Appendix A, and assign privilege codes where applicable. Any uncertainty about responsiveness or privilege should be escalated via “Needs Further Review” or “Needs Priv Review.” Training on Relativity and the case background is mandatory, and quality control checks will monitor coding consistency.

Relevance criteria:
Documents are relevant (responsive) if they relate in any way to: (1) the City of Atlantis RFP or municipal energy project; (2) BigThorium’s process for bidding on contracts, especially this project; (3) discussions among BigThorium employees about giving gifts, incentives, or luxury goods as part of the bid or contract process; (4) discussions about bribing, influencing, or otherwise improperly inducing City of Atlantis officials; (5) communications about BigThorium winning or seeking to win the City of Atlantis contract; (6) purchase, reimbursement, or delivery of luxury goods or other items that could serve as bribes; (7) communications between City of Atlantis officials and BigThorium representatives; (8) contractual discussions, negotiations, or execution of the municipal energy project contract; (9) mentions of the City of Atlantis by BigThorium employees in the context of the project or contract; and (10) any expression of intent or plan to influence the award of the municipal contract. Relevant documents may include emails, texts, memos, presentations, invoices, expense reports, travel records, or notes. Privileged documents can still be relevant and must be coded for privilege separately. Documents are non-relevant if they do not concern the City of Atlantis, the municipal energy project, or BigThorium’s bidding and gift practices—for example, materials about unrelated clients, projects in other cities, general marketing, or internal HR issues with no connection to the RFP or alleged bribery. Routine business communications that mention energy projects but not the City of Atlantis or the relevant time frame, and documents solely about unrelated procurement processes, should generally be coded Not Responsive.
