### 2.9 California decision treating GenAI review as TAR (P15)

- **Schulte v. LinkedIn Corp., No. 22-cv-00237-HSG (LB) (N.D. Cal. June 30, 2026)**, U.S. Magistrate Judge Laurel Beeler. Twelve-page discovery order; plaintiffs challenged LinkedIn's use of Relativity aiR for responsiveness review; the court treated aiR as a form of TAR under the operative ESI protocol, allowed search-term pre-culling, and declined to compel elusion rates, error rates or reviewer counts absent a specific deficiency.
- Arnold & Porter, eData Edge, July 2026, https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/blogs/edata-edge/2026/07/court-declines-to-give-generative-ai-review-special-scrutiny : "the court treated that tool as a form of Technology Assisted Review (TAR), not a novel technology requiring its own rules ... Schulte v. LinkedIn Corp., No. 22-cv-00237-HSG (LB) (N.D. Cal. June 30, 2026)."
- WilmerHale, "Old Rules, New Tools", July 20, 2026, https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260720-old-rules-new-tools : "the decision indicates that courts will treat generative AI review as a new form of traditional Technology Assisted Review (TAR) rather than a novel category of discovery."
- ComplexDiscovery, https://complexdiscovery.com/federal-magistrate-judge-treats-linkedins-relativity-air-workflow-as-tar/ : "Beeler signed the order June 30, 2026, and it entered the docket the next day. It creates no precedent that binds another court"; notes retired Judge Andrew Peck and Reema Holz (DLA Piper) describe it as "the first federal decision to accept generative AI making the final responsiveness call in discovery."
- Paul Hastings, https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/when-the-ai-is-the-reviewer-schulte-and-the-emerging-rules-for-generative-ai-in-document-review ; ACEDS blog, https://aceds.org/you-cant-compel-what-you-didnt-negotiate-schulte-v-linkedin-and-the-limits-of-ai-transparency-in-discovery-aceds-blog/ (see Flags: ACEDS reports a contrary N.D. Cal. ruling one week earlier).
